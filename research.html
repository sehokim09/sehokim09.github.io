Ionospheric remote sensing

ECIPES: Tsunami detection

Ships at sea continuously broadcast their position over the Automatic Identification System (AIS), a VHF radio signal. At these low frequencies the signals are highly sensitive to the ionosphere's electron content. ECIPES uses AIS broadcasts as signals of opportunity to sense the ionosphere, with the goal of detecting the ionospheric disturbances that tsunamis generate as they travel across the ocean.

Land remote sensing

Root-zone soil moisture

Root-zone soil moisture controls crop growth, drought and the carbon cycle, but it lies below the depth most satellite sensors can see. Signals of opportunity reflectometry (SoOp-R) reuses existing communication and navigation satellite transmissions across many frequencies. Lower frequencies penetrate deeper into soil and vegetation, so combining several of them makes it possible to retrieve soil moisture profiles and vegetation water content together.

Our work spans retrieval algorithms and sensitivity analysis, constellation and mission design (the open-source SOCRATES simulator), and spaceborne demonstration with NASA's SNOOPI P-band CubeSat.
